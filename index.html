MODELING THE HUMAN EAR, 2025

The outer ear plays a critical role in shaping how we perceive sound—but how can we model the complexity of conductive sound in these tissues? For my senior thesis, I built a multilayer physical model of the human ear designed to replicate the material and geometric properties of soft tissue, cartilage, and bone.

MEMS TRANSDUCER, 2025

Expanding on the concept of delivering sound through mechanical conduction, I designed a microelectromechanical systems (MEMS) designed to operate at a fraction of the size and power consumption of commercial devices. By leveraging MEMS fabrication techniques, I developed a proof-of-concept device that can discretely intergrated into wearable technologies for conduction hearing applications.

CELL ENGINEERING, 2025

The scope of bioengineering spans many length scales, from molecular interactions to tissue-level organization. However, collective cellular behaviors—such as migration, coordination, and wound healing—emerge at the scale of individual cells interacting with their immediate physical environment. Framing cells as foundational building blocks enables systematic investigation of how physical constraints, geometry, and substrate organization regulate these behaviors.

CO-CONTRACTION EXTENDO ARM, 2023

The Extendo Arm is a wearable assistive device designed to augment human reach and interaction through intuitive biomechanical control. Activated via muscle co-contraction, the system translates natural neuromuscular signals into mechanical extension, enabling the user to engage the device without external controllers or conscious mode switching.
