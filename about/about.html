HEY! I'M ALYSSA.



I am a recent graduate from Harvard College. I studied bioengineering working to build on the intersection of biomaterials and augmentation of the human body. Much of my time has been spent in tissue engineering– particularly on fabricating human vascular structures from scratch.

However, my recent interest in wearable device applications led me to pivot towards large scale human interfaces. My senior thesis project involved a collaboration with Mass Eye and Ear, where I worked to fabricate a realistic model of the human outer ear to investigate cartilage-mediated sound transmission.

I built EUNGYO STUDIOS (pronounced "UNG-yoh") as a cohesive space for all of my work and interests. From the website to every illustration, image, and video, everything here is created by me from scratch. The portfolio brings together technical projects from school and self-initiated projects.

I LIKE TO BUILD THINGS.


It took until the second semester of my junior year of college to realize why I had subconsciously chosen to pursue engineering in the first place. Growing up, I was endlessly curious and drawn to learning by doing—from learning origami at age five, to joining weekly knitting circles to gossiping with my retired seniors friends at age 12. Making things has always felt instinctive to me.


The first time I was introduced to electronics and digital fabrication was during a final project for BE124, Biomechanics of Movement and Assistive Robotics, where we were tasked with building a device based on principles from the course. My group members, coming from stronger computer science and mechanical engineering backgrounds, quickly dove into the technical components. I had promised my teamates that I would handle the biology aspects of the project, but building a wearable device meant electronics became quickly unavoidable for my role. At some point, an Arduino and breadboard was handed to me and I was left to figure out the rest.


I was scared at the thought of letting my teamates down, but I was also excited for the challenge. I took it upon myself to catch up. After all, teaching myself new skills was nothing new. Through countless YouTube videos and trial and error, I learned how to place the electrodes correctly, write code to process EMG signals, and calibrate for thresholds that would work across different users. This work culminated in the Co-Contraction Activated Extendo Arm project (2023), developed in collaboration with my project partners—and now lifelong friends—Mackenzie Li and AnhPhu Nguyen.

During that same semester, I was introduced to photography through an elective course. Once again, I found myself reconnecting with the joy of working with my hands—this time in a more artistic way. Naturally, I was drawn to film photography due to the analog process of the medium. But it wasn't until I decided to build my own camera and understand its mechanics that I truly let myself dive into film. This kickstarted my 서Eungyo project (2023‐present), where I built a 35mm camera from scratch and have been shooting with it ever since.


Together, this was the semester when everything clicked.

When I returned to school, I promised myself I would intentionally seek out courses that allowed me to keep building and creating. I enrolled in Introduction to Digital Fabrication (PS70), an elective that formally introduced me to the makerspace world. There, I learned how to CAD, use machining tools, code, and digitally document projects.

I became a course assistant for PS70 the following semester, supporting students with their own projects through office hours.

Since then, I have worked on a range of projects that involve building physical systems. Long story short, this website documents my journey of embracing my love for building and creating—through the projects I have worked on, and the ones I will continue to pursue.

HOBBIES, ETC.


Again, outside of academic and project work, I gravitate toward hands-on creative pursuits. I enjoy experimenting, building small things, and following ideas wherever they lead.


When I step away from making, I'm usually outside. Growing up in rural southeastern Ohio meant spending long stretches of time outdoors—camping, hiking, cycling, swimming. That rhythm of movement and quiet has stayed with me.
