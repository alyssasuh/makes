Expanding on the concept of delivering sound through mechanical conduction, I designed a microelectromechanical systems (MEMS) designed to operate at a fraction of the size and power consumption of commercial devices. By leveraging MEMS fabrication techniques, I developed a proof-of-concept device that can discretely intergrated into wearable technologies for conduction hearing applications.

ES276, Microelectromechanical Systems, is a graduate-level course offered at Harvard that covers the design, fabrication, and application of MEMS devices. For my final project, I designed and fabricated a MEMS transducer that can convert electrical signals into mechanical vibrations for bone conduction hearing. On this page, I document the design process, fabrication steps, and testing results of the MEMS transducer.
